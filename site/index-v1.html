Your phone is the most capable computer you own. It is also the one you are least allowed to program.

Calls are still a 1970s interface

A missed call is a lost patient, order or opportunity. Voicemail is where messages go to wait. Nothing on the phone can pick up and help on your behalf.

Apps are locked in walled gardens

You cannot tell Android or iOS to "summarise my WhatsApp every evening" or "answer unknown numbers and screen them". The pieces exist; the phone won't let you wire them together.

The AI can finally keep up

Full-duplex voice models now listen and speak at once, in real time, for about $0.05 a minute. Android 13 added system APIs for call audio. The missing part is the layer in between. That is Rethink.

    How it works

    Rethink sits where the call audio lives.

    Most AI phone products forward your number to a server somewhere. Rethink runs on the handset, inside Android, between the modem and the speaker. Your number, your SIM and your phone stay exactly as they are.

    It answers like you would

    When a call rings and you don't pick up, Rethink answers, attaches to the call's audio and mutes the handset microphone so nothing in the room leaks through.

    It listens and talks at once

    A full-duplex voice model hears interruptions mid-sentence. For anything that needs work, such as a lookup or a booking, it hands off to a reasoning model with tools and keeps the caller company while it waits.

    It only claims what it did

    Messages are saved and calls are ended by real tools that report back. The assistant is told never to say it did something until the tool confirms it. You get a summary on your phone the moment the call ends.

    The hard part

    Why this hasn't been done on a normal phone

    Android has blocked apps from call audio since Android 10. Android 13 added two system-only APIs for call-assistant features, but they need a privileged system app, and whether they work depends on each phone's audio driver.

    On this phone, the official injection API reported success while the caller heard nothing. The audio policy dump showed why: call-assistant audio is routed to a voice_tx port that the Qualcomm driver never mixes into the uplink. Rethink requests the in-call music path instead, which the driver does send to the modem.

    This kind of device-level knowledge is what the platform is built on. Each new phone we support starts with the same probe: dump the routes, test both directions on a live call, then ship.

    CallBridge.java · attaching to a live call// Caller → Rethink: the official downlink API works as documented.
AudioRecord rec = audio.getCallDownlinkExtractionAudioRecord(PCM_16K_MONO);

// Rethink → caller: on this Qualcomm audio driver the call-assistant
// track lands on voice_tx and never reaches the modem. A media track
// that explicitly asks for TELEPHONY_TX gets INCALL_MUSIC instead.
AudioTrack track = new AudioTrack.Builder()
    .setAudioAttributes(usage(USAGE_MEDIA), speech())
    .setAudioFormat(PCM_16K_MONO)
    .build();
track.setPreferredDevice(device(TYPE_TELEPHONY));

audio.setMicrophoneMute(true);   // the AI owns this call: no room echo
    Programmable

    Answering calls is the first extension. You write the next one by asking.

    Rethink turns the phone's abilities into functions a program can call: calls, messages, notifications, calendar, location, apps on screen, the web and the AI itself. Tell Rethink what you want, and it writes a small, readable extension, shows you what it can touch, and runs it.

    Rethink for Business

    One phone or a whole reception line

    A phone can only hold one call at a time. That is fine for a person and not enough for a busy clinic. So the extension runtime is designed to run in two places with the same code: on the handset for individuals, and in Rethink Cloud for business numbers that need several calls at once, a web dashboard and practice-software connectors.

    Owners set it up by talking: "I'm a dentist in Indiranagar, here's my price list, root canals take an hour." Rethink writes the rules and the knowledge base, then calls the owner for a practice run before going live.

    Principles

    An assistant you can trust with your phone.

    It says it's an AI

    Every call opens with a clear disclosure. Recording and data handling follow consent and India's DPDP Act.

    You can always take over

    Join any call the AI is handling, or set which callers it should never answer.

    Extensions show their reach

    Each extension declares what it can touch. A shared news digest never gets to read your WhatsApp.

    Readable, not magic

    Extensions are short scripts you can read, edit and share. Nothing about your phone is hidden from you.
